Independent project · June 2026–present

PvZH Vault

A website for Plants vs. Zombies Heroes players to find decks, build their own, and understand the game.

Visit PvZH Vault Recognition code

I started PvZH Vault because deck information for the game was scattered and hard to use. I collected, cleaned, and organized raw data into a database of more than 2,000 decks, then built the tools I wanted as a player. Other players started using them too.

Some of the harder parts

Screenshot recognition
I built a computer-vision and OCR pipeline that identifies cards in a screenshot and turns them into an editable deck.
Automatic deck building
Algorithms that put together decks using hero restrictions, card combinations, costs, and strategy rather than choosing cards at random.
Making the data useful
Collecting and transforming raw game and deck data into search, statistics, card synergies, guides, and features for the community.

PvZH Vault is an independent fan project and is not affiliated with EA or PopCap.

Independent project · Working prototype

RibbonReady

Software to help someone open a restaurant in the City of St. Louis without having to figure out every permit and form themselves.

Visit RibbonReady

A restaurant opening involves company registration, taxes, permits, inspections, and documents that depend on one another. RibbonReady keeps track of that process and does as much of the paperwork as possible instead of handing someone a checklist.

I've been building workflows that reuse information across filings, prepare application documents, handle prerequisites, and let owners work with contractors and other professionals in one place. Some government handoffs are still simulated in development.

Interesting engineering problem: representing a messy real-world process as dependable software, including the steps that can't happen until another approval comes through.

Visit Starfin

The starting point is a SaaS product for the teams running creator campaigns. We're talking with creators, brands, and agencies to learn where existing tools fall short and which workflows are actually worth improving.

There's also a financial side we're interested in: faster payouts for creators and underwriting that better accounts for creator income. Those are goals we're exploring, not features we're claiming to have launched.
